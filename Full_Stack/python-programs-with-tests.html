Summary

CSE 111 focused on writing clean, reusable functions and proving they work with automated tests. These two programs show that approach best.

Molar mass calculator

chemistry.py asks for a chemical formula such as C6H12O6 and a sample mass. It parses the formula, including nested parentheses, with a dedicated formula.py module, looks each element up in a periodic-table dictionary, and reports the molar mass and number of moles.

def main():
    formula = input("Enter the chemical formula of the sample: ")
    mass = float(input("Enter the mass of the sample in grams: "))
    periodic_table_dict = make_periodic_table()
    symbol_quantity_list = parse_formula(formula, periodic_table_dict)
    molar_mass = compute_molar_mass(symbol_quantity_list, periodic_table_dict)
    print(f"Molar mass: {molar_mass:.5f} grams/mole")
    print(f"Number of moles: {mass / molar_mass:.5f}")

Water distribution pressure model

water_flow.py models the pressure at a house supplied by a city water tower. It accounts for water column height, friction loss in pipes and fittings, the Reynolds number, and pipe-diameter reduction. Each physics step is its own function with its own tests.

def test_pressure_loss_from_pipe():
    assert pressure_loss_from_pipe(0.048692, 0, 0.018, 1.75) == approx(0, abs=0.001)
    assert pressure_loss_from_pipe(0.048692, 200, 0.018, 1.75) == approx(-113.008, abs=0.001)
